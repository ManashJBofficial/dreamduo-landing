**Start with one concrete question about everyday life.** Try “What would you want a normal weekday to look like for us a year from now?” Each answer before discussing what overlaps. Write down one thing you agree on and one decision you need more time or information to make.

You can use these questions if you're dating, considering a move, or revisiting plans you've already made. Pick three that fit what you're deciding. Ask a follow-up when the other person's answer surprises you.

## Everyday life and time together

1. What would you want an ordinary weekday to feel like a year from now?
2. How much time would you like us to set aside for ourselves each week?
3. What's one routine you'd like us to share, even when we're both busy?
4. What should a weekend together include when we're both tired?
5. What would tell you that work has started to take too much of our time?

**Follow-up:** Ask for an example from last week. If “more time together” means dinner for one person and uninterrupted conversation for the other, you can plan for both.

## Home and where to live

6. What would you miss most if we moved away from where you live now?
7. What would make a place feel like home to you?
8. How close would you like to live to the people you rely on?
9. What would you want us to agree on before we shared a home?
10. If we couldn't live in the same place next year, what would help the waiting feel manageable?

If you're already discussing a move, use the [moving-in checklist](/blog/moving-in-together-checklist-for-couples) to work through day-to-day questions about space, chores and expenses.

## Work, money and practical choices

11. What work commitment or personal project do you want me to make room for this year?
12. What shared expense would you like us to plan before it becomes urgent?
13. Which parts of our money would you want to manage together, and which separately?
14. What spending decisions would you want to talk about first?
15. What would make dividing household work feel fair during a busy month?

## Experiences and goals

16. What would you like us to try together that neither of us has done?
17. What small tradition would you be happy to repeat for years?
18. What do we keep saying we'll do that we could start this month?
19. What's one goal you'd like me to encourage without managing it for you?
20. If a big plan had to wait a year, what smaller version would you still enjoy?

## Decisions that need more time

21. Which of our plans feels clear to you, and which still feels uncertain?
22. What timeline have you been picturing for our next big decision?
23. What are we assuming the other person wants without having asked?
24. What would you need to learn before deciding?
25. When should we come back to this, and what could each of us bring to that conversation?

## Save one decision and one open question

Copy these lines into a shared note. Use your own words for anything that matters to you.

1. **What each of us wants.** “I'd like an easier commute.” “I'd like to stay close to friends.”
2. **Where we agree.** “We both want regular time together during the week.”
3. **What we're still deciding.** “We haven't picked where to live.”
4. **Who takes the next step.** “I'll list two areas we'd both consider and their usual commute times.”
5. **When we'll talk again.** “Sunday after lunch.”

If you disagree, reflect what you heard before arguing for a choice. “I heard you say that being near friends matters more than having extra space. Did I get that right?” You can leave the decision open and still agree on what to find out next.

## What if you're ready on different timelines?

Imagine one person wants to move this spring and the other has six months left in a training program. Ask what each date would make possible. One person might need a clearer idea of what the waiting period looks like. The other may want time to finish without rushing.

Write down what has to happen before either of you decides. For example, check the program's end date and agree on one visit while you wait. Set a date to review the information together. That gives you something concrete to work on while the larger decision is open.

If a serious topic catches one person off guard, ask for a time to come back to it. You can use the [weekly check-in worksheet](/blog/15-minute-weekly-relationship-check-in-12-questions-template) to make room for both answers.
