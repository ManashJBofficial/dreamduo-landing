## Questions 1–5: sexual attraction and feeling wanted

These ask directly about desire: what turns you on, when you feel wanted and what your partner might not have noticed.

<EditorialPhoto collection="intimacy" id="desire" />

### 1. What do I do that turns you on, not just makes you feel loved?

Invite an answer about desire, rather than only affection.

### 2. When you're thinking about me during the day, are you ever thinking about sex with me?

This works as a direct opening over text when you both want that kind of conversation.

### 3. When was the last time you wanted me badly and didn't say anything?

Talk about what made it easy or difficult to say so.

### 4. What outfit, look, or move of mine gets you going that I might not even know about?

Let your partner name their own preferences.

### 5. What would make you feel wanted by me, sexually?

Keep the answer specific enough that you can understand it while you are apart.

## Questions 6–10: flirting while you are apart

For texts and private calls, start by agreeing that you both want to flirt. Choose a question that suits the moment.

<EditorialPhoto collection="intimacy" id="apart" />

### 6. What's the dirtiest text you've wanted to send me but didn't?

Decide first what kind of messages you both want to receive.

### 7. Is there a way you wish I'd tease you when we can't do anything about it?

Distance makes this question relevant; agree what feels enjoyable and what does not.

### 8. Would you rather chase me sexually, or have me chase you?

Discuss who likes to initiate and how you each show that the attention is welcome.

### 9. When did I last make you horny in a totally normal moment?

Your partner might remember something you did not realise had that effect.

### 10. What's a line you'd want me to whisper to you on a private call?

This adapts the bank's whispering question to a long-distance conversation.

## Questions 11–15: intimate memories you still think about

These are for couples with shared physical memories. Skip ahead if you have not met in person yet.

<EditorialPhoto collection="intimacy" id="memories" />

### 11. Have we had a sexual moment that was perfect and we never recreated it?

Discuss what mattered about that moment, without expecting the next visit to copy it exactly.

### 12. What's a moment between us that you've replayed alone since it happened?

Choose only a memory you want to share.

### 13. Is there something you've done with me that you loved but acted casual about?

This gives you a way to name a preference that might otherwise stay unspoken.

### 14. Is there an era of our sex life you wish we could get back?

Talk about what you miss and what is different now.

### 15. When did my hands on you last get you worked up?

If you have not met yet, skip the shared-history questions and move to preferences.

## Questions 16–20: fantasies and curiosity

You can discuss a fantasy without wanting to try it. Keep that distinction clear as you share.

<EditorialPhoto collection="intimacy" id="fantasies" />

### 16. Is there a fantasy of yours I've never heard out loud?

Sharing a fantasy does not commit either person to acting on it.

### 17. What have you been curious about sexually but afraid I'd judge?

Listen before responding, and let your partner choose how much to say.

### 18. Which fantasy of yours is more about the feeling than the act itself?

This can open a conversation about the meaning behind a fantasy.

### 19. Have you ever wanted to try something kinky and not known how to bring it up?

Treat this as a conversation about interest and boundaries, not an agreement.

### 20. What's the fantasy you think about most when you touch yourself?

Use this only if you both want a more personal conversation. Passing is enough.

## Questions 21–25: saying what you want during sex

Distance can give you time to talk about preferences before you are together again. Let each person decide what they want to share.

<EditorialPhoto collection="intimacy" id="preferences" />

### 21. Is there something specific you want me to do in bed that you've never asked for?

Make room for a preference you have not discussed before.

### 22. What makes it hard for you to say what you want during sex?

Ask what would make speaking up easier.

### 23. Is there feedback about my technique you've been holding back?

Listen to the feedback without turning it into a score for either person.

### 24. What's one thing you've thought during sex with me and never said out loud?

Your partner decides whether this is something they want to share.

### 25. What would I have to do for you to tell me anything sexual, with zero shame?

Focus on what an accepting response looks like to each of you.

## Questions 26–30: intimacy on your next visit

Talk about what you miss and what you want to explore together, while leaving room for how you both feel when the visit arrives.

<EditorialPhoto collection="intimacy" id="reunion" />

### 26. Where on your body do you wish I touched you more, sexually, not just sweetly?

Talk about preferences now, then check what feels right when you meet.

### 27. Is there a kind of foreplay you've been missing from us?

Use the answer to understand each other, rather than make a fixed itinerary.

### 28. What kind of kissing does it for you, deep, slow, hungry, with teeth?

Different preferences are fine; discuss what each person enjoys.

### 29. If we had a 'just sex' weekend with nothing else on the agenda, what would yours look like?

Treat this as an imagined conversation, not a demand on the visit.

### 30. Do you feel safe telling me you're not in the mood without me reading into it?

End with room for an honest answer. Desire is not an obligation.

## How to ask sexual questions over text or a call

Start with a simple invitation: “Do you feel like talking about desire tonight?” If the answer is yes, choose two or three prompts together.

- **Take turns.** Share your own answer, rather than only interviewing your partner.
- **Leave room for timing.** A late reply may mean work, sleep or no privacy. It does not tell you how much someone desires you.
- **Separate sharing from agreeing.** Naming a fantasy or preference does not mean promising to try it.
- **Keep personal conversations personal.** Agree before saving messages, taking screenshots, recording calls or sharing intimate content.

The [love is respect guide to digital boundaries](https://www.loveisrespect.org/resources/digital-boundaries/) gives useful starting points for discussing what you are comfortable sharing online. You can have a sexual conversation without sending photos or recording anything.

## Make it a short conversation you want to repeat

You do not need a complicated game. Pick one question about attraction, one about a preference and one about your next visit. Ask a follow-up only if your partner wants to keep talking.

If something important comes up, give it time instead of rushing to the next prompt. If you disagree, you can leave the idea there. The goal is understanding each other's sexuality, not getting a particular answer.

## Common questions

### What are good sexual questions for long-distance couples?

Ask what makes your partner feel sexually wanted, what they miss about your physical intimacy, what preferences they have not mentioned and which fantasies they feel comfortable discussing. The questions above offer direct prompts for each of those conversations.

### Can spicy questions work over text?

Yes, if both adults want that conversation. Send one prompt at a time, share your answer and let your partner reply when they have privacy. Agree on what you are comfortable sending and saving. You do not need to include photos.

### How do we talk about fantasies without pressure to try them?

Say that you are discussing curiosity, not making an agreement. Ask whether the fantasy is something your partner enjoys imagining, something they might want to discuss further, or something they want to keep private. Neither person has to act on an answer.

### What if we have different sexual preferences?

Listen to the difference without treating one preference as the correct one. Discuss what both people want and where the boundaries are. There may be a shared interest to explore, but either person can also say no.
