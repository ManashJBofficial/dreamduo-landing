## How to start without putting your partner on the spot

Ask whether they have privacy and feel like a flirty conversation. If the answer is yes, send one question and answer it yourself too. That makes the exchange mutual rather than asking them to carry the mood alone.

If a reply is short or uncertain, ask whether they would prefer another time. For help putting a preference into words, read the [Gottman Institute's guide to expressing sexual desires](https://www.gottman.com/blog/how-to-feel-comfortable-expressing-sexual-desires-with-your-partner/).

Their preferred tone matters more than the label. Ask about their experience instead of assuming which kinds of attention a boyfriend or girlfriend should enjoy.

## Common questions

### Which question should we start with?

Choose one you both want to discuss rather than treating the numbering as a required order. All 35 use explicit adult language; either person can skip a question or stop.

### Can these questions make someone feel attracted to me?

No question can guarantee attraction or a particular mood. These prompts help existing partners discuss what they enjoy and how they like to be approached. A clear no, a change of subject or a request to pause is enough to stop.

### What if my partner doesn't want to answer?

Let the question go. You can ask whether they'd prefer a lighter topic, but they don't owe you a replacement answer. For a less personal conversation, try our [30 daily questions for couples living apart](/blog/30-daily-questions-for-long-distance-couples-when-you-run-out-of-things-to-say).
