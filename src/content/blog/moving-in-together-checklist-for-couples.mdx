**Before moving in together, agree on what you can each contribute, what you'll bring, and how you'll share the work.** Write your decisions somewhere you can both see. Give moving-day tasks an owner and schedule a short check-in after your first week.

Whether one person is moving into the other's home or you're both moving somewhere new, use this checklist in stages. If you're still deciding whether to move, start with the [questions about your future together](/blog/questions-to-ask-your-partner-about-your-future-together).

## Before committing to the move

- **Choose a realistic move date.** Account for work, travel and any overlap between homes.
- **List one-time and recurring costs.** Include moving, basic supplies, utilities and the expenses that will repeat. Agree who pays what and when you'll review it.
- **Discuss what each person needs at home.** Compare sleep times, work calls, guests, pets, storage, quiet and private space.
- **Decide what to bring.** Make a keep, donate, store or replace list for duplicates. Check measurements before moving large furniture.
- **Decide who handles each recurring task.** Include buying supplies, arranging repairs and remembering appointments.

If one person is joining an established home, walk through it together and agree on what can change. “Which shelf would you like to make yours?” is easier to answer than “Do you feel at home yet?”

### Talk through a normal week before moving day

Try describing an actual Tuesday. Does someone need a quiet room for an early call? Who gets home first? When do you each want visitors? Write down the two things most likely to interrupt the other's routine.

For example, say Ari takes calls from home and Sam works evenings. They agree to keep the bedroom free for sleep after a late shift and put a desk in the living room. They try that arrangement for a week before buying a divider or permanent furniture.

## Two weeks before moving day

1. **Confirm access.** Write down key pickup, building instructions and where the vehicle can stop. Check whether you need to reserve an elevator.
2. **Arrange transport.** Confirm pickup time, box count, contact numbers and who meets the driver at each address.
3. **Set up essentials.** Confirm when heat, water and internet will be ready. Make a backup plan if either person needs to work on the first day.
4. **Update important contacts and deliveries.** Change addresses directly with the organizations that send important mail. If you're moving in the US, use the [official USPS forwarding guide](https://www.usps.com/manage/forward.htm) to check what it can reroute.
5. **Pack a first-night kit.** Keep chargers, toiletries, bedding, medication, a change of clothes and basic kitchen supplies together. Put the kit where you can reach it without unloading everything.

Give each task one owner. Both of you can help, but one person needs to know whether it has been done. For example, “Sam will confirm key pickup by Thursday; Ari will call about the internet by Wednesday.”

## Moving day and the first evening

- Keep keys, documents and the first-night kit with you.
- Mark boxes by the room they'll go into, then unload the essentials first.
- Check that both partners can get in and know where to find the basics.
- Choose a stopping time for unpacking. Make the bed, eat something and save the rest for later.

## Review after the first week

Ask what worked, what surprised you, and what one change would make next week easier. Keep the answer specific. If one person keeps discovering the kitchen is out of supplies, decide who checks the list and when they do it. Try the change for a week before revising the whole arrangement.

Start with the part of the week you most want to improve:

- **Space:** “Is there somewhere you can work or unwind without interruption?”
- **Chores:** “Which task took more effort than we expected?”
- **Expenses:** “Did we miss anything in the monthly plan?”
- **Time:** “What should we put on the calendar for just the two of us?”

The [15-minute check-in worksheet](/blog/15-minute-weekly-relationship-check-in-12-questions-template) gives you a structure for that conversation.

## Keep one list for everything still open

Keep each task on a line with an owner, date and status. For example, **“Measure the bedroom window. Ari, Saturday. Still open.”** Once the measurement is recorded, mark that task done and give ordering the blinds its own date. Before closing the list, decide which tasks can wait a month.
