**Start with WhatsApp for calls and voice notes, Google Calendar if time zones keep tripping you up, and one shared place for your next visit.** A Google Keep list can handle a few tasks. DreamDuo makes more sense when you're tracking a trip or goal over several weeks and want the steps, links and progress together.

DreamDuo publishes this guide. The six picks below cover different jobs, and we link to each provider so you can check what works on both phones before you set it up.

## 1. WhatsApp for calls and voice notes

[WhatsApp](https://www.whatsapp.com/) is a good default if you already use it on your phones. The same chat gives you somewhere to leave a voice note when you miss each other and somewhere to make a call when your hours overlap.

**Try it on a busy day.** Send a quick note while walking home: “I passed that bakery we talked about. Tell me when you're free and I'll show you what I found.” If your partner won't be off work until you're asleep, they can answer when it's convenient. Save a longer story for the call if you'd rather hear their reaction.

**Where it gets awkward.** A message like “book the earlier one” is useful in the moment. Two weeks later you may have to scroll past dozens of messages to find which train it meant. Once you decide something that affects a booking or a deadline, put it on your shared plan.

## 2. Google Calendar for two time zones

[Google Calendar shows invited guests the event in their own local time](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37064?hl=en). Start with one event you both want to keep. You can add the other person's city as a secondary time zone if you'd like to see both clocks while scheduling.

1. Pick a time that fits around shifts, meals and sleep. A short call both people can make is a better starting point than an ambitious daily schedule.
2. Add your partner as a guest and include the call details in the event. Each check the start time on your own phone.
3. Agree on a backup before you need it. For example, if one person gets held up at work, send a note and use the backup slot on Sunday.
4. Recheck the next event when clocks change or one of you travels. The local time can shift for one person even if the event stays fixed in the other person's zone.

The [guide to long-distance routines in different time zones](/blog/long-distance-relationships-in-different-time-zones-3-weekly-routines) has worked schedules for three-, six- and ten-hour differences.

## 3. Google Keep for a shared list you can set up today

A [shared Google Keep note](https://support.google.com/keep/answer/6101196?hl=en) lets both people edit a list. It suits a visit that has a few straightforward decisions and no need for separate milestones.

Start a note called **Next visit**. Under it, write “Confirm Friday arrival,” “Check whether we can host,” and “Pick Sunday breakfast.” Put someone's name next to each item. Add links beneath the relevant task. When you find out the Friday train arrives too late, update that line instead of leaving the change in a chat.

Check that your partner can edit the note before relying on it. Google notes that deleting a shared note you own also deletes it for the people you shared it with, so copy any details you still need before removing it.

## 4. DreamDuo for a visit or goal that takes several weeks

[DreamDuo](/download) is useful when a list turns into a project. You can set up a shared checklist goal, keep links and files with it, and record progress. An amount goal can track money you've manually set aside for the trip. Use your usual accounts to handle the money itself.

**Example: plan the next visit.** Under a goal called “Weekend together,” one person checks two travel options while the other confirms the dates they can host. Save the travel links with the goal. After you book, add the check-in time and decide when to review what's left. If the budget is still open, use the [visit checklist and budget worksheet](/blog/how-to-plan-your-next-long-distance-visit-free-checklist-budget) to settle the detailed costs.

For something smaller between visits, try a daily question. Each partner answers privately before both replies are revealed. You can also send a nudge, such as a quick hello or a poll about the next movie night.

## 5. Paired for a guided conversation

[Paired](https://www.paired.com/) offers daily questions and quizzes for couples. It's an option when your calls reliably happen but most of them end up being updates about work, chores or the next visit. Check what both partners can access before paying for a plan.

**Try one question at a time.** Say one of you answers in the morning and the other replies after work. When you talk, pick one part of the reply worth asking about. “You said you miss doing boring errands together. Which one would you actually want to do on our next visit?” gives the conversation somewhere to go. If you're choosing between two dedicated apps, see the [DreamDuo and Paired comparison](/compare/dreamduo-vs-paired).

You can also try this routine in a chat using the [free 30-day question calendar](/blog/30-daily-questions-for-long-distance-couples-when-you-run-out-of-things-to-say).

## 6. FaceTime and SharePlay for a watch date

If you both use compatible Apple devices, [Apple's SharePlay guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/shareplay-watch-listen-play-iphb657eb791/27/ios/27) explains how to watch supported video in sync during a FaceTime call. Both people need access to the title, which may mean separate subscriptions or purchases.

Pick a short episode and have both people open it before the call. Catalogs can differ by country, even when you both use the same service. If the title isn't available to one of you, pick something accessible on both devices and count down to play, or choose a [video date that needs no streaming subscription](/blog/25-things-to-do-on-facetime-with-your-partner-when-you-re-bored).

## What a week with two time zones could look like

Take two partners in London and Bengaluru in late November. A Saturday call at **2:00 p.m. in London** is **7:30 p.m. in Bengaluru** when London is on GMT. When London switches to summer time, check the actual invite again before reusing those times.

1. **Monday:** One person creates the call in Google Calendar. The other confirms it leaves time for dinner and chooses a Sunday backup.
2. **Tuesday:** Each leaves a short WhatsApp voice note when they have a minute. They agree to catch up on the notes by the weekend.
3. **Thursday:** In their shared trip list, they each bring one option. One checks travel times. The other labels two possible places to stay. They agree to book only after they settle on dates together.
4. **Saturday:** They spend the first ten minutes deciding what to do about the trip. They leave the rest of the call free to talk or watch something.
5. **Sunday:** Each says which part of the setup they used. If neither opened the extra app, they keep the shared list in the tool they already use.

## Do both partners need the same app?

For a shared goal or a paired question feature, follow the app's invitation flow and make sure both people can open it. For a calendar event, try one invitation before setting up a whole shared calendar. Check how notifications work on both devices, especially if one person sleeps while the other is active.

If you're only solving one problem, set up just that part of the week. For a missed call, start with a calendar event. For a trip with too many separate screenshots, start with a shared plan.
