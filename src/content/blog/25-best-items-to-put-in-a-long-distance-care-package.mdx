A good **long-distance care package** brings a little of your world into your partner's hands: snacks from your hometown, a scarf with a familiar scent, a hoodie they love borrowing, or a photo from your last visit. Choose things they can wear, taste, use or keep close. A letter or photo can add meaning, but the box can hold much more than paper.

## What are the best items to put in a long-distance care package?

Start with **something familiar, something they enjoy and something personal**. For example, pack your hoodie, a few regional snacks and a framed photo of a day you spent together. Choose the contents around your partner's tastes, not a generic hamper.

These 25 ideas include main gifts and small extras you can combine into one parcel. Pick a few that belong together rather than trying to include everything.

## Choose a care package by occasion

| Occasion | A simple combination | Keep in mind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A small everyday surprise | A photo, a letter and a favorite snack | Set a total limit including delivery. |
| A difficult week | Something comforting, a useful item and a short note | Ask what would help rather than assuming. |
| A birthday or anniversary | One main gift with a personal keepsake | Check the arrival window before ordering. |
| An international parcel | Lightweight, durable items with personal meaning | Check official customs and carrier rules for the destination. |

**Packing checklist:** Choose a few items your partner will use, check food ingredients and destination restrictions, protect anything fragile, confirm the address, and include a note. Weigh the packed box before choosing delivery so postage doesn't become the surprise.

## Personal and romantic care package ideas

### 1. A framed photo of your favourite moment together

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-01" />

Choose a photo that means something to both of you: a first trip, a reunion at the airport or a day you still talk about. Put it in a small freestanding frame they can keep beside their bed or on their desk. Write the story behind the picture on the back so the gift carries more than an image.

**Put it together:** One photo print, a lightweight frame and a short note about the moment. Choose a picture you are both comfortable displaying, and protect the frame for delivery. An acrylic-front frame can be easier to post than glass.

### 2. A small set of “open when” letters

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-02" />

Choose three or four occasions such as “when you miss home,” “when something goes well” and “when you need a laugh.” Keep each message complete on its own so opening it does not create another task.

**Put it together:** labelled envelopes with notes, a favourite memory and an optional photo. Avoid rules about when they are allowed to read them.

### 3. Printed photos with a story on the back

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-03" />

Choose a handful of pictures rather than sending every photo in your camera roll. Write the memory, date or small detail that is not obvious from the image.

**Put it together:** a few prints protected between pieces of card, with a note saying which one you would put on your desk. Pick photos you are both comfortable having printed and delivered.

### 4. A tiny book of one shared memory

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-04" />

Make a short booklet about one trip, your first date or an ordinary weekend you loved. A folded handmade version works; it does not have to be a professionally printed album.

**Put it together:** a few photos or drawings, captions and an empty final page for something you want to do next. Keep the booklet small enough to fit the chosen package.

### 5. A card that opens a private voice message

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-05" />

Record a short message, a funny story or a goodnight wish. Put a link or QR code on a card, with a written version of the important part in case the link does not work.

**Put it together:** a card and a tested link that your partner can access. Check the sharing settings and whether the link expires; do not use a public recording for something private.

## Useful gifts for everyday life and your next visit

### 6. Their favourite chocolate or sweets

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-06" />

Include the chocolate, chewy sweets or biscuits they always choose when you are together. Remember the exact flavour they like, whether that is dark chocolate, sour sweets or something you shared on your first date. A familiar treat makes the box feel chosen for them.

**Put it together:** Unopened treats in their original packaging. Check ingredients, allergies and dates, and choose something that will travel well rather than melt or spoil.

### 7. Your hoodie, with a note in the pocket

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-07" />

If they are always borrowing your hoodie when you meet, send it to them for the weeks apart. A familiar item of clothing can carry a shared memory that a new generic gift does not. Tuck a note in the pocket explaining when you next hope to wear it on a walk together.

**Put it together:** A hoodie or sweatshirt they have said they like, freshly washed and folded, with a pocket note. Use one you are happy to give them, check that it fits, and avoid adding perfume or fabric fragrance unless you know they want it.

### 8. A candle for your next video-date night

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-08" />

Choose a candle in a scent your partner already enjoys, or an unscented one if they prefer that. Add an invitation to a quiet evening on a call: music, a drink you each choose and time to catch up. You can keep a matching candle at home to give the two evenings a small detail in common.

**Put it together:** One candle, a personal invitation and a link to your evening playlist. Check their fragrance preferences, the carrier rules and how to protect the container during delivery. Use the candle only where they can keep it attended and away from flammable items.

### 9. A travel pouch for your next reunion

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-09" />

Make the next visit feel a little closer with a compact wash bag they can use on the journey. Choose a colour or style that fits their luggage, and slip in a note about the first thing you want to do when they arrive. It is a practical gift with a clear connection to seeing each other again.

**Put it together:** A travel pouch, a toothbrush or another small item they would use, and your welcome note. Choose the toiletries with them rather than guessing skincare preferences. Check mailing rules before adding liquids; an empty pouch with a note can be the gift on its own.

### 10. An instant camera for memories between visits

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-10" />

If your partner enjoys taking photographs, an instant camera lets them collect little moments to share with you later: the view on their walk, a new cafe or their favourite corner at home. Suggest bringing a few prints to your next visit so you can add them to a shared album. Choose a camera only if photography is something they enjoy.

**Put it together:** An instant camera, compatible film and a card with a few optional photo ideas. Check the exact film format and allow for the cost of replacements. Confirm the carrier rules for the camera and batteries if you plan to post it internationally.

## Local treats, familiar scents and shared favourites

### 11. Local snacks from your hometown

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-11" />

Share the flavours you grew up with or the snacks you keep telling them about. Choose regional crisps, packaged sweets, biscuits or a savoury snack they cannot easily find where they live. Add a mix of your own favourites and something you think they would enjoy trying.

**Put it together:** Two or three sealed local snacks, with a short note about where each one comes from or when you usually eat it. Keep ingredient labels attached and check what food is allowed at the destination before posting.

### 12. A scarf with your familiar scent

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-12" />

A scarf you wear can feel personal in a way a new accessory does not. Choose a clean scarf they would enjoy wearing or keeping nearby. If they love your usual perfume, a very light touch of it can make the gift familiar; let it dry fully before packing, and skip added fragrance if they prefer it that way.

**Put it together:** A soft scarf, folded neatly in its own wrapping, with a small favourite treat alongside it. Keep it separate from food so the fragrance does not transfer. Check the carrier rules for scented items, and do not assume a perfume bottle can be mailed with it.

### 13. A small game that works over a call

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-13" />

Choose a compact game or make your own simple guessing cards. Check how you will play when the cards are in only one person's hands; not every tabletop game works remotely.

**Put it together:** a tested set of cards, a short explanation and a suggestion for your next call. Keep the first round short so it feels like a date rather than homework.

### 14. A tiny creative project to do at the same time

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-14" />

Try paper folding, sketching or making postcards for each other. Choose an activity where neither person needs to be good at it, and make room for different results.

**Put it together:** lightweight supplies, a prompt and a photo of your own attempt if you want. Avoid adding paints or liquids without checking the shipping rules first.

### 15. A playlist with handwritten track notes

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-15" />

Choose a short playlist rather than a hundred songs with no explanation. Add a sentence about a few tracks: a memory, something you want to dance to, or a song they introduced you to.

**Put it together:** a card with the playlist link and your notes. Test the link on an account your partner can use, since service access and track availability can differ by country.

## Birthday, anniversary and milestone packages

### 16. A birthday countdown with three wrapped surprises

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-16" />

Instead of one birthday parcel, wrap three small gifts inside it and suggest opening one on each of the two days before their birthday, then the last on the day. Choose a favourite treat, a photo keepsake and one item from their wish list. Put a personal birthday message with the final gift.

**Put it together:** Three individually wrapped gifts labelled with the suggested opening dates, plus a birthday card. Choose each surprise around something they like. Send early using the carrier's current estimate, and make the countdown optional if the parcel arrives late or they prefer opening everything together.

### 17. An anniversary timeline

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-17" />

Choose a few moments from your relationship and explain why each matters. Include ordinary milestones as well as official dates: a difficult conversation you handled well or the first recipe you cooked together.

**Put it together:** a folded timeline, a few photos and an invitation to add their favourite moment. It can be entirely handmade.

### 18. A comfort care package for a hard week

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-18" />

When they are facing exams, long shifts or a difficult week, send things they can enjoy straight away: a favourite snack, something soft to wear and a short, reassuring note. The purpose is to make a quiet moment nicer, without giving them an activity to organise or a project to finish.

**Put it together:** One ready-to-use comfort item, a permitted treat and a note such as "I know this week has been a lot. This is for whenever you get a moment to yourself." Ask what would help if you are unsure, and let them enjoy it without expecting a call, unboxing photos or an immediate reply.

### 19. A first-day-at-work gift kit

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-19" />

Celebrate a new job, internship or promotion with something useful for their first day. A notebook and pen can become a personal keepsake when the first page holds a message about how proud you are of them. Mention the effort you saw: the applications, interview preparation or evenings spent learning something new.

**Put it together:** A notebook, a pen, a congratulations card and an optional favourite treat. Choose the notebook size and style for how they work; initials on the cover are optional. If dates are uncertain, present it as a gift for their new chapter rather than promising delivery before a particular morning.

### 20. A small reminder of your next visit

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-20" />

Send a card with one thing you would like to do when you meet. If dates are not booked, describe it as an idea rather than presenting an uncertain trip as a promise.

**Put it together:** a personal invitation, a photo of a place you want to share and one practical little item for the visit. Keep travel bookings and important documents in your usual secure channels.

## Small personal extras to tuck into the box

### 21. Postcards from the places you pass every day

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-21" />

Send a glimpse of your routine: a local street, a nearby park or a photo you printed yourself. Add what you would show them if they were walking beside you.

**Put it together:** a small set of cards and a letter. Ask the postal service whether your envelope qualifies as correspondence; adding goods can change the service and customs requirements.

### 22. Bookmarks with a different message on each

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-22" />

Make a few slim bookmarks for different moods. One can hold a memory, another a joke, and another a note for the next time they open a book after a long day.

**Put it together:** sturdy paper, handwritten messages and an envelope or protective sleeve. Rounded corners and flat decoration keep them usable.

### 23. A personalised keyring or bracelet with their name

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-23" />

Choose a keyring or bracelet made with your partner's name, your initials or a meaningful date. Engraved metal, wire-letter keyrings and letter-bead bracelets offer different ways to make it theirs. You can also add a short message, such as "Always with you" or a few words from a private joke.

**Put it together:** one personalised keepsake in a small protective pouch, with a short note explaining the name or message you chose. Check the spelling and personalisation options before ordering. For a bracelet, choose a material and size they would enjoy wearing; for a keyring, keep it light enough for everyday use.

### 24. Stickers or paper extras chosen for their interests

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-24" />

Choose a small set around their favourite animal, hobby or running joke. A few well-chosen stickers are more personal than filling the package with things they will not use.

**Put it together:** flat stickers, a note explaining your choices and a small notebook if they would use one. Confirm they like decorating things before buying a large set.

### 25. Paper invitations to future adventures

<EditorialPhoto collection="gifting" id="gift-25" />

Make two or three invitations to simple things you want to do together: a sunrise walk, a homemade breakfast or a visit to a place they love. Leave room to pick the date together.

**Put it together:** invitation cards and one note about the plan you are most excited for. Keep them as offers, not obligations or rewards your partner has to earn.

## How to put your care package together

1. **Choose the main gifts.** Start with something personal, such as your hoodie or scarf, then add a local treat and a keepsake they would enjoy.
2. **Pack the items separately.** Keep scented fabric away from food, leave snacks sealed, and cushion mugs, frames and other fragile gifts.
3. **Add one personal touch.** A short note about why you chose the contents is enough; let the gifts carry the rest.
4. **Check the parcel before sending.** Confirm the address and your carrier's rules for the actual contents, especially food, fragrance and batteries. For US-origin international parcels, [USPS lists restricted items](https://www.usps.com/international/shipping-restrictions.htm).

## Care package questions

### What can I put in a long-distance care package on a small budget?

Combine a handwritten note, a printed photo and a favorite snack, or include a familiar item you already own. Set a total budget that includes packing and postage before buying extras.

### Can I send food in an international care package?

It depends on the food, origin, destination and carrier. Check the destination's official customs rules and your carrier's restrictions before packing. Sealed packaging alone does not make an item eligible to ship.

### What should I write in a care package note?

Explain one reason you chose the contents and mention a moment you look forward to sharing. A specific sentence about your partner means more than filling a page with generic quotes.
