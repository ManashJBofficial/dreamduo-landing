## Brief 1 — shared goals app for couples

- **Primary intent:** commercial investigation / choosing an app for a shared practical plan. This is inferred from the query wording and sample product results, not measured demand.
- **Proposed final search title:** Shared Goals App for Couples | DreamDuo
- **Suggested URL:** `/shared-goals-app-for-couples`
- **Meta description:** Plan shared goals as a couple with DreamDuo. Track tasks, savings progress, habits and milestones, with photos and links beside your plans.
- **H1:** A shared place for your goals as a couple.
- **Distinct value:** show one complete, realistic plan rather than a generic feature list: name the goal, choose its type, agree the next task, add progress and reach a milestone. Explicitly explain that amount goals record progress; they do not connect bank accounts or move money.
- **Verified features to demonstrate:** amount/checklist/habit/time dreams, shared or private goals, related tasks, progress and milestone messages, saved files/links. Use the current app's terminology and confirm each depicted screen before publication.
- **Actual assets:** `/blog/best-couples-apps/dreamduo-dreams-screen.webp`, `/dreamduo-milestones-phone.webp`, and `/dreamduo-files-phone.webp`. Label screenshot examples and do not fabricate the completion of an illustrative plan. Reserve dimensions and load secondary images lazily.
- **Suggested outline:**
  1. One shared goal, with the next action visible to both of you.
  2. A worked example: planning a weekend visit, using the genuine goal and milestone screens.
  3. Pick a goal type that matches the task: amount, checklist, habit or time.
  4. Keep practical details beside the goal: tasks, notes, files and links.
  5. Shared vs private plans, and inviting your partner using the current app flow.
  6. Accurate free/Pro boundaries and who might prefer a shared note instead.
  7. Short factual questions about platforms, bank connections and getting started.
- **Internal links in:** contextual links from the shared-goal how-to, visit checklist and relevant app comparisons; avoid replacing every existing download link with a new detour.
- **Internal links out:** existing shared-goal how-to, visit checklist, `/compare`, `/download`, privacy policy.
- **Conversion CTA:** “Start one shared goal” with both existing configured store badges. Use named positions such as `shared_goals_hero` and `shared_goals_end`; preserve campaign URL configuration and the existing event dispatch path. Explain what the visitor will do after installing, without promising an automatic pairing.
- **Publish gate:** inspect query/page overlap for “app”, “tracker” and “how to” variants; compare representative US/India/UK results based on actual audience data. If the current homepage/how-to already satisfy the same task, enhance them rather than create a second target. Confirm partner invitation and privacy screenshots against the shipped app.

- **Meta description:** Discuss your future together with 25 questions about everyday life, home, work and shared plans. Choose one next step using a simple planning worksheet.
- **H1:** 25 questions to ask your partner about your future together

- **Relevant features:** daily questions for reflection; shared dreams/checklists for a plan the couple explicitly chooses. The proposed prompts are editorial content, not a promise they exist in the app's daily bank.

  3. Home, location and the kind of life you want (five prompts).
  4. Work, time and practical priorities (five prompts).

## Repeatable operating workflow

1. **Establish receipt and definitions first.** Check GA4 event delivery, register dimensions, and calculate Organic Search session conversion using `blog-conversion-tracking.md`. Confirm each event fires once before making a rate comparison.
2. **Export an actual baseline.** Use the same date windows, landing pages, device/country filters and consent/reporting settings. Separate branded and non-branded queries. Record clicks, impressions and qualified store-click sessions; keep app-console installs and backend activation definitions separate.
3. **Review intent and effort.** Prioritize a demonstrated product task and unique visitor value. Keep a simple decision log: query evidence, existing-page owner, source claims, genuine assets, maintenance owner and last source-check date. One focused page is preferable to a batch of thin variants.
4. **Publish incrementally after review.** Start with the best-supported commercial brief. Compare landing-page cohorts and CTA positions over a sufficiently populated period; low-volume tap changes are not an A/B-test result. Watch cannibalization and changes in query mix before attributing movement to a title or CTA.
5. **Compare downstream outcomes honestly.** Store visits are website events. Use store-console acquisition data where available, then authorized aggregate onboarding/pairing/shared-dream reports if definitions exist. No current user-level join from a blog tap to an activated couple was established.
6. **Maintain sources.** Recheck competitor feature/pricing references on a regular editorial cycle or when a user reports a discrepancy; retain storefront/date/duration caveats. Review field CWV and actual crawler/indexing signals before choosing technical optimizations.

Success is more qualified couples reaching a useful app experience, not an impression target alone. The three briefs remain hypotheses until demand and distinct intent are validated.
